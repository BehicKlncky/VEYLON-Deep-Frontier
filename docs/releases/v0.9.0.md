# VEYLON 0.9.0 — The Abandoned City

A new world is now an abandoned city. You wake in a small refuge and live on what
its buildings still hold: every one of them can be entered and searched, the
infected hunt by sound, an alien growth spreads through the quarantine, and the
people who stayed remember how you treat them. Hold a refuge, help or rob the
survivors and bring the old radio relay back, without felling a tree or mining an
ore vein. The wilderness of earlier releases stays on offer as the legacy world.

## What changed

- **A city that covers the world.** A connected metropolis streamed region by
  region, with six districts: Reclaimed Residential, Commercial Downtown,
  Industrial Yards, the Flooded Quarter, the Civic Precinct and the Quarantine
  Zone. Districts change the architecture, what the buildings hold, the hazards,
  the creatures and the climate. Parks, canals and courtyards are part of it.
- **Every building is a real space.** Walk-up apartments, corner shops, grocers,
  clinics, schools, offices, security posts and warehouses have furnished rooms,
  stairs, exits and searchable storage, proven reachable before they are built.
  Basements, service passages, transit stations under the avenues, fire escapes
  and rooftop routes connect what the streets do not. Doors open, lock, bar and
  break; windows shatter.
- **Scavenging with a cost.** Contents stay hidden until a search ends; every
  search takes time and makes noise; what is taken never comes back. A pry tool
  forces locks loudly. Survivors' stores are theirs, and taking from them is theft.
- **Threats.** The Veilbound, colonists changed by an alien membrane, notice you by
  sound and in the light and pursue you through doors and up stairs. The Veilmaw
  grows from the quarantine's nodes and telegraphs its strike; fire withers its
  growth. The Breacher hammers through plank barricades. Skitterwings, Murkhares
  and Glowdeer live in parks, courtyards, canals and on roofs.
- **People.** Small survivor camps talk, trade and give missions. Civilians hide
  in sealed cellars behind loose panels and can be heard before they are found.
  Guarded strongholds keep their gates, their rules and their stores; a raider
  yard takes what it can. Help, theft and violence are remembered.
- **A home.** The starter refuge has a bed, a workbench and a day of supplies.
  Claim up to three flats, barricade doors and windows, and take furniture and
  burnt-out cars apart for salvage. Taps hold only the water left in their pipes;
  a generator fed from intact fuel tanks runs work lamps, a water purifier or a
  radio on its building's circuit. Dying wakes you at your refuge with what you
  carried.
- **A long goal.** Ask survivors about the old radio relay, recover its three parts
  across the districts, earn its codes or scan the band for them, repair it and
  broadcast from a radio at your refuge. The city answers, and the game goes on.
- **Finding your way.** `M` opens a city map of the streets you walked, the
  buildings you entered, your home, the sites you found and your objectives, with
  a journal and a sketch of the storey underfoot. The HUD names the district,
  building and floor; prompts say what a search or a lock costs.
- **Look and sound.** Walls stop light, interiors stay dry, districts are dressed
  and marked, toxic fog shows where it harms, and rooms, halls, basements and
  tunnels each sound like themselves, with a district soundscape and the threats'
  tells.
- **NEW FRONTIER chooses the world.** It opens on the city; `W` switches to the
  wilderness, labelled as the legacy world; `S` and `C` choose Survival or
  Creative as before. Creative works in the city too.
- **Bounded long journeys.** A city drops its farthest chunks and regenerates them
  block for block; each chunk is meshed once, from a finished neighbourhood.

## Save compatibility

The save format remains binary v3, with one slot shared by every world and mode.

- **Older saves in 0.9.0:** saves from 0.2.0 to 0.8.1 open in their own world, the
  wilderness, with its terrain, settlements and rules unchanged. This is checked
  against genuine saves written by the archived 0.3.0 and 0.8.1 builds, in Survival
  and Creative.
- **0.9.0 saves in older releases:** a wilderness world saved by 0.9.0 gains only
  optional sections and opens in 0.8.1, as the archived 0.8.1 build showed. A city
  save names a world generator that releases up to 0.8.1 do not know; they refuse it
  before touching their live world, and the file is not changed.
- **City saves** keep what changed: searched and broken containers, doors, windows,
  walls, barricades, the dead, people and their needs, claimed homes, water, fuel
  and power, discoveries, missions and the relay. The streets, buildings and people
  are re-planned from the seed. A damaged city section fails the whole load and
  keeps the last good save.

Fires, panic, AI routes and open searches' screens are not saved, as before.

## Known limits

- The day's temperature curve is inverted in every world: 14:00 is the coldest hour.
  It is unchanged so existing worlds keep their outcomes.
- A city plans each region the first time you come near it; fast travel into
  unplanned streets can hitch briefly. Saving a city takes a little longer the more
  places it has recorded. There is no autosave.
- The city has no gardening: beyond what its buildings hold, food and water come
  from rain, the taps' last water, purifiers and trade.
- The release smoke streams no long approach in the city; city streaming is
  measured by its own native tour.

## Validation and packages

On 2026-11-06, `gradlew.bat releaseArtifacts --rerun-tasks --no-daemon
--console=plain` passed on Windows 11 Pro x64 with Temurin 25.0.4.1+1, a Ryzen 5
5600X and an RTX 3060 Ti: **2365 tests in 305 classes, no failures or errors**, with
one skip: the isolation check for a symbolic link to the player's directory, since
this host may not create symbolic links. This includes Javadoc/doclint, the fat JAR
and the Windows app-image ZIP. The archive was checked for its executable, bundled
runtime, versioned JAR, license, Windows natives and required launcher options, and
both the fat JAR and the unpacked archive's `Veylon.exe` passed a 30-second release
smoke in the city (1,026.1 and 1,015.5 FPS, save and load, zero GL and KHR errors).

`gradlew.bat performanceTest --no-daemon --console=plain` passed the city profile (5
tests), the rain gates (2) and nine of ten benchmarks. The durable-save gate failed
at 5.295 ms against 2.20 ms, the gap this host has shown since 0.7.4 (5.872 ms at
0.8.1); load timing was not reached. No budget was relaxed, and the reference
machine has not run this release.


Natively, on the release's code, at 1280 x 720 with isolated data directories and
zero GL and KHR errors throughout: the default new-world route from the title over
two launches (a new city, a search, F5, a death and the wake at the refuge, F9, the
pause screens, quitting, then loading from the title) passed in Survival and
Creative; the mission tour (camp, hidden civilians, stronghold, utilities, relay
and the city's answer) and the quarantine tour passed on the default world; and the
release smoke passed in the city (1,036.9 FPS), the Creative city (1,057.0) and the
wilderness (1,491.3, its fortress approach 1,574.5), VSync off. NEW FRONTIER was
captured at 1280 x 720 and 1920 x 1080 with interface scales 0.75, 1 and 1.5.
The owner played the city before release.

The tag workflow builds and validates Windows x64, macOS Apple Silicon and macOS
Intel archives, then publishes them with `SHA256SUMS.txt`. Each archive includes
Java. macOS builds are unsigned and not notarized; macOS packaging is validated by
CI rather than by the local Windows checks.
