# VEYLON 0.9.1 — Safe Saves

A release that protects your progress before the next round of content. Three save slots
with rotating backups, a checksum on every save and autosave at safe moments make progress
hard to lose. NEW FRONTIER takes a seed of your own, the pause menu leads back to the
title, and the mouse can be tuned. The defects earlier releases recorded and left behind
are fixed, among them a day that was coldest in the afternoon.

## Saves from 0.9.0 do not open in 0.9.1

**Saves written by VEYLON 0.9.0 and earlier do not open in this release.** Start a new
world to play 0.9.1.

Why: 0.9.1 changes what a save is. Every save now begins with a **save epoch**, a number
that says which worlds and states a save can hold, and carries a checksum and a short
summary that the slot picker reads without loading anything. A build opens only saves of
its own epoch, and 0.9.1 is the first, epoch 1. The project's save policy since 0.9.0 is
that only saves written by the current version must open, so 0.9.1 drops the readers and
migrations for the layouts of earlier releases instead of carrying them forward; what it
keeps is strict reading of its own saves.

What happens to an old save:

- It is refused before anything is loaded. The slot picker shows it as written by an
  older version of VEYLON, F9 says so in the log, and the world you are in stays as it was.
- The file is left exactly as it is: never changed, moved, imported or deleted. 0.9.0 kept
  one save, `saves/veylon.sav`; the slot picker mentions it and never opens it.
- To go on playing an old world, keep 0.9.0 installed beside 0.9.1. The two can share a
  saves folder: 0.9.0 reads only its own `veylon.sav` and never sees the slots, and given a
  0.9.1 save in its place it refuses it before touching its world and leaves the file as
  it is.

Epoch 1 is now recorded as released: a later release that changes anything a save holds
raises the epoch, and will then refuse 0.9.1 saves the same way.

## What changed

- **Three save slots.** LOAD FRONTIER lists them with what each holds (the world and mode,
  the day and time, the time played, where you stood, when it was saved and by which
  version) or says it is empty, from another version or damaged, without loading a world.
  NEW FRONTIER asks which slot the new world goes into and saves it there at once; a slot
  that holds a save asks before it is replaced. F5 saves into the world's own slot, F9 loads
  it, and the pause menu names it.
- **Backups and damage.** Each slot keeps the two saves before its current one. A save is
  written in full before anything is replaced, so a failed save keeps the slot and both
  backups as they were. Damage anywhere in a save is caught by its checksum; a damaged slot
  offers its newest good backup, and restoring it keeps the damaged save beside it.
  Deleting a slot asks first.
- **Autosave**, on by default: after you wake from sleep, after you claim a refuge at a bed
  and every 10 minutes of play. It waits for a safe moment, never saving while you are
  hurt, hunted, falling, burning, asleep or in a menu, shows SAVING... just before it and
  keeps the save it replaces as the newest backup. `A` on the title or in the pause menu
  turns it off or sets 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes.
- **Seeds.** NEW FRONTIER shows the seed the new world will be built from, a new one each
  time; `E` types your own, a number or any words, and `R` rolls another. The pause menu
  names the world and its seed, so a world can be shared.
- **Back to the title.** `R` in the pause menu asks first: `Enter` saves into the world's
  slot and returns, `N` returns without saving and leaves the slot as it was, `Esc` stays.
  Everything of the world is let go, so the next one starts as after a fresh launch.
- **Mouse.** CONTROLS on the title and `C` in the pause menu set the mouse sensitivity from
  10% to 300% of the earlier turn and invert the mouse's Y axis; both are kept for the next
  launch.

## Fixed

- The day is warmest in the afternoon and coldest in the night before dawn, in the city
  and the wilderness; until now 14:00 was the coldest hour. The industrial yards are hot
  enough by day to make you thirsty, as their arrival line warns, and nights are colder
  everywhere, so a roof and a fire count for more after dark.
- A save made just after a killing shot no longer brings the person or animal back
  standing with no health, and a person shot dead as you leave their settlement counts as
  killed, with everything that follows a death.
- A whole carcass keeps the arrows lodged in it through a save and load.
- A captive in a fort's cage stands on the cage floor instead of hanging in the roof bars,
  and a leader is set down on the keep's floor.
- The death card after a blast that blew you apart watches your remains fall from a little
  behind and above, instead of from inside them; health on the death card reads 0 after an
  overkill hit, and no block name shows through the death card, the city's answer or the
  pause menu.
- Leg pieces of a body blown apart no longer come to rest standing on their ends, nothing
  alive breathes or sways while the game is paused, and quivers, slung bows and guns hang
  at their angles again.
- A burning log, plank or leaf block shows its fire on its faces open to the air.
- A city's pause menu no longer calls the game a development build, and leaving a world
  for the title no longer leaves its fires, shots, body pieces, log or camera shake behind.

## Known limits

- Autosave waits for a safe moment, so a long fight, swim or Creative flight holds it until
  it ends. Saving stays on the game's main thread: a large city's save is a short stall,
  announced by SAVING..., not a background write.
- A backup is offered only when its slot's save is damaged or missing, and damaged saves
  set aside by a restore stay in the saves folder until you remove them.
- The slot picker, NEW FRONTIER and the pause menu use the working names "The abandoned
  city" and "The wilderness" for now.
- A city plans each region the first time you come near it, so fast travel into unplanned
  streets can hitch briefly, and saving a city takes a little longer the more places it
  has recorded.
- The city has no gardening: beyond what its buildings hold, food and water come from rain,
  the taps' last water, purifiers and trade.

## Validation and packages

On 2026-11-27, `gradlew.bat releaseArtifacts --rerun-tasks --no-daemon --console=plain`
passed on Windows 11 Pro x64 with Temurin 25.0.4.1+1, a Ryzen 5 5600X and an RTX 3060 Ti, in
5 min 56 s: **2526 tests in 345 classes, no failures or errors**, with two skips, the isolation
checks that need a symbolic link, which this host may not create. This includes
Javadoc/doclint, the fat JAR and the Windows app-image ZIP. The archive was checked as the tag
workflow checks it (the executable, the bundled runtime, the versioned JAR, the license, the
Windows natives and the launcher's options), and the fat JAR and the unpacked archive's
`Veylon.exe` each passed a 30-second release smoke in the city with their working directory
set to an empty folder (1,007.9 and 991.2 FPS, save and load, zero GL and KHR errors). The
whole build takes about 6 minutes on this host, down from 18 at 0.9.0.

`gradlew.bat performanceTest` passed the rain gates and nine of ten benchmarks; the
durable-save gate failed at 5.255 ms against 2.20 ms, this host's gap since 0.7.4 (5.295 ms at
0.9.0). The city profile passed in two of three runs; in the other, its flight leg's p99 frame
was 0.04 ms over its half-frame gate, an edge the tagged 0.9.0 build reaches on this host too.
No budget was relaxed, and the reference machine has not run this release. The release smoke
in the city and the wilderness (995.1 and 1,419.1 FPS, VSync off) measured the same as the
tagged 0.9.0 build on the same afternoon, and the Creative city smoke, whose flight now starts
above the roofs, passed at 1,038.8 FPS.

Natively, with isolated data directories and zero GL and KHR errors throughout, the release's
acceptance scenario passed from the title in the city and the wilderness, in Survival and
Creative: a new world of a typed seed, a search, a night's sleep and the autosave after it, a
second world saved with F5, both ways back to the title, both slots loaded exactly as saved, a
save written by the 0.9.0 build refused and left unchanged, the mouse settings kept across a
relaunch, and a damaged slot restored from its backup. The tagged 0.9.0 build refused a 0.9.1
save and left it unchanged. The evidence of every fixed defect and of the corrected day was
run again on this release.

The verify workflow builds every push on Windows x64, and its weekly macOS Apple Silicon build
passed as well. The tag workflow builds and validates Windows x64, macOS Apple Silicon and
macOS Intel archives, then publishes them with `SHA256SUMS.txt`. Each archive includes Java.
macOS builds are unsigned and not notarized; macOS packaging is validated by CI rather than by
the local Windows checks.
