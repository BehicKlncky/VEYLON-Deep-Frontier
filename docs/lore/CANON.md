# VEYLON Canon — Lore Baseline

**Status: canon v1, written at the owner's request and approved by the owner on 2026-11-19.**
The owner approved every section and chose option A for the wilderness world kind (§12). That
answers the names-and-lore question of urban decision P-01; [§15](#15-approval-record) holds the
record. Player-facing text still uses working names in places. Later work replaces them when it
changes that text, and new content uses this file as its reference. The canon changes display
text only. Stable identifiers, save ids and enum names never change because of lore.

Labels used below:

- **CANON**: approved by the owner. A change needs the owner's approval; new names that follow
  §11's rules do not.
- **OPEN**: deliberately unanswered. Records may hint at it; nothing may settle it.

## 0. How to use this file

- Read it before you write anything a player reads: names, signs, notices, records,
  dialogue, map labels, item names. Also read it before you add a district, a building type or
  a landmark: the story decides what each place looks like, what is left in it and who is there.
- Mechanics come first. If a mechanic and this file disagree, the mechanic wins. Record the
  conflict and put the correction of this file to the owner; do not invent an exception in text.
  [§9](#9-what-the-lore-must-explain) lists what the lore has to account for.
- VEYLON is not a story game. There are no cutscenes, no required reading and no chosen one.
  The story is underneath: in the shape of the city, in what was taken and what was left, in
  signs, notes and the way people behave. A player who never reads a record must still be able
  to play the game fully. A player who reads every record must still have questions.

## 1. Pillars

1. **An ordinary city.** Varrow was a working city with groceries, gun shops, schools,
   libraries, bus stops and laundromats. The horror comes from ordinary places that people
   left in a hurry.
2. **The story is in the world.** Looting patterns, locked flats, evacuation notices, door marks
   and found records tell what happened. No narrator.
3. **Lore explains mechanics.** Each rule the player learns has a reason inside the world, for
   example why a bite always infects or why the antiserum cannot be made.
4. **A mystery at the centre.** Nobody knows what the Veil is. People have theories, and none of
   them is confirmed.
5. **Original.** Names, factions, creatures, text and imagery are VEYLON's own (urban R9).

## 2. The setting on one page

Veylon is a temperate world at the far end of the colonial shipping lanes: the Deep Frontier.
Sixty-one years ago a charter colony landed in a river basin ringed by highlands and built
**Varrow**: a brick old town on the Sorrel River, a downtown of towers, neighbourhoods, suburbs,
an industrial belt, and farms that ran out to the forests of the Rim.

To power and water a growing city, the colony drilled the **Deepworks**, a deep geothermal bore
under the industrial Works. At depth the bore met something that had always been in Veylon's
stone: a thin, living film the colonists came to call **the Veil**. The first people it took were
the bore crews. Within a month the medical quarter was walled off as **the Cordon** and then
overrun, the city was evacuated over the **Highline Pass**, and the colony's own council sealed
the pass behind the last train. That kept the Veil inside the Basin, and it kept everyone who had
not left inside too.

About a hundred and forty days later, the city is quiet. The **Veilbound**, people the Veil has
taken, walk the streets they used to live on. Small camps of **stayers** live in apartments and
cellars. A remnant of the city's guard, the **Wardens**, holds the civic quarter. Salvage gangs,
the **Wreckers**, strip the industrial yards. The old **radio relay** that was Varrow's only voice
to the outside has been silent since the pass closed.

The survivor is one of those who stayed. They are nobody special, and the game does not need
them to be.

**Native life.** Veylon's life is chemically close enough to Earth's that most of it can be eaten
after cooking. Colonists named native animals after the Earth animals they resembled (wolves,
deer, boars, crows). Scholars complained and everyone else did it anyway. That is why the game's
animals have Earth names. Deep caves hold stranger things (the wilderness's gloomstalkers).

**The Veil beneath** (OPEN in its nature, CANON in its behaviour). Deep under the soil, in
fissures and wet stone, lies a thin film of living tissue. It is slow and dormant and grows far
below any foundation. For sixty years nobody knew it was there.

## 4. The colony and the city

### 4.1 The Charter

The colony was founded under **the Varrow Charter**, named for the ship *Varrow* that brought the
first nine thousand settlers. An elected **Charter Council** governed from **Charter Hall**.
Three Charter rules shaped everyday life and still shape the game:

- **Everything must be repairable from colony stock.** Varrow ran on sturdy, slow technology:
  radios and landlines, paper ledgers and tape recorders, diesel engines, generators, hand tools.
  That is why records are paper and tape, why radios matter, and why salvage can rebuild a
  generator or a purifier.
- **The Arms Act.** Civilians may own black-powder arms for hunting and defence against
  wildlife: muskets, flintlocks and blunderbusses, sold with powder and shot in licensed gun
  stores. Cartridge weapons such as the *Frontier Carbine* were Warden issue only, so
  cartridges are rare and kept in secured armouries.
- **One voice.** All contact beyond the Basin went through the city's relay. The relay office
  answered to the Council.

## 6. The Veil

### 6.1 What people saw

- **The sheen**: a thin, wet, faintly iridescent membrane on dark, damp surfaces, such as cellar
  walls, tunnel ribs and courtyard ground.
- **Nodes**: knotted growths that anchor the sheen. Fire kills them.
- **The haze**: the heavy fog that the densest growth breathes out. It sickens anyone who breathes
  it, and it pools in the Cordon.
- **Veilmaws**: mouths that the sheen grows beside a node. They are not people (§6.3).

### 6.2 How it takes a person (it must match owner rules I1–I3)

The taken carry Veil filaments in their mouths and under their nails.

- **A bite** drives filaments into the blood. A bite always takes (I1). Nobody has ever recovered
  from a bite without the antiserum.
- **A scratch** leaves residue in the wound. Antiseptic kills it. Left alone, it takes hold in
  roughly one wound in seventeen (I3's 6 %).
- **The course** takes about three days (I2's set time):
  1. *Bitten.* The wound and the bleeding.
  2. *Fever.* The body fights and loses: heat, thirst, weakness.
  3. *Veining.* Dark threads under the skin and at the edge of sight. The Veil reaches the lungs,
     and the cough begins. It is a rasp that the taken hear and come to, as if it were a call.
  4. *Turning.* Blackouts as the Veil reaches the brain.
  5. *Taken.* What walks away is no longer the person.
- **Death while infected** does not stop it: the body rises taken.

### 6.3 The taken and the Veil's other forms

- **Veilbound**: people the Veil drives. They keep habits but not selves. They walk old routes,
  stand in their own kitchens and hunt by sound and sight, and they see best in light. They keep
  hold of whatever they were carrying, which is why a **former self** still has its pack (I4).
  Where many people lived, many were taken. That is why the towers and the old blocks are
  dangerous and the farms are quiet.
- **Breachers**: the bore crews and the heaviest labourers, taken first and taken longest. The
  Veil thickened them. They keep their trade's habit and hammer at boards and braces until
  something gives.
- **Veilmaws**: not people. Each is a mouth that the sheen grows beside a node. It waits folded
  and never leaves its node's ground. Placed light draws it, so it creeps to a torch and smothers
  it, and fire drives it back. Kill the node and it withers.
- **What hurts the Veil**: fire burns the sheen, the nodes and the growth's edge. Antiseptic kills
  fresh residue in a wound. The antiserum works in the blood, but only before turning. Nothing
  helps a body after it has turned.

### 6.4 The antiserum

In the last two weeks before the Cordon fell, the Tessaly Institute made an antiserum from the
blood of the three bore workers who held out longest. It works if given before the turning. The
Institute made a few hundred doses in small batches, the **Tessaly series**, and sent most of them
to the Cordon's field clinics and the city's secured pharmacy dispensaries. Then the Institute
was overrun. Nobody outside it knew the method, and the cultures it needed died when the power
went off.

**Every dose that exists already existed on Day 30.** This is why the antiserum is only ever
found, never made, bought, traded or given, and why it is very, very rare (I2). Look in
secured medical storage. Records may say so, and none may point to a particular cabinet.

### 6.5 What the Veil is (OPEN)

People in the city hold four theories. Records may voice any of them. None may be confirmed.

1. **The skin of the world.** Veylon is alive, the Veil is its skin, and the bore was a wound.
2. **The sleeper.** A dormant organism that the bore's heat woke.
3. **Tessaly made it worse.** A rumour that the Institute's work spread the Veil. Wreckers
   repeat it.
4. **The singing.** The bore hummed on the relay's frequencies before the first case, and some
   say the Veil listens.

## 8. The relay and the answer

The relay mast is Varrow's only voice to the Port and to the Long Lane. Its crew left on Day 31
with the codes, and the Wardens hold some of its parts. When the survivor brings it back, the
band scan picks up fragments: Warden channels, Wrecker chatter, the Port's automatic beacon on a
loop, and now and then something else (OPEN; see §6.5, theory 4).

The answer (CANON, for 0.13.0): the Highline replies, the first sign that some evacuees reached
safety. If survivors reach a rooftop evacuation point and hold it through one night, a lifter
will come once. The game does not end there, and play continues.

## 10. The story beneath the map

World generation tells the story. These rules guide 0.9.3, 0.9.4 and later content:

1. **Who took what, and when.**
   - Days 0–19: panic buying. Supermarkets, pharmacies and gun stores near the centre were
     emptied first, so they are the most often picked clean.
   - The Warden sweeps forced doors in Old Varrow.
   - The Wreckers stripped downtown offices for electronics and the Works for metal.
   - Specialty shops, suburban garages and farms are more often intact, though they hold less.
2. **The evacuation left a trail.**
   - Suitcases dropped by doors, and notices pasted on them ("Evacuation: fairground. One bag
     each.").
   - Cars abandoned on the arterials, and the jam on the Highline Road.
   - The staging camp at the fairground, and the overrun checkpoint at the foot of the pass.
3. **Shelters.** Schools and churches were used as shelters: cots in gyms, aid stations in
   halls.
4. **The Cordon.**
   - Sheet-metal walls and decontamination posts.
   - Field clinics where the antiserum was sent.
   - The densest sheen and haze.
5. **The stayers' marks** are by the doors of the places they mean.
6. **Record arcs** for found records (0.12.0). Each arc is told in pieces across districts:
   - *The Bore*: Deepworks logs.
   - *Tessaly*: case notes and the antiserum batches.
   - *The Cordon*: Warden logs.
   - *The Last Trains*: evacuation papers.
   - *Sealing the Pass*: Council radio.
   - *Stayers*: family notes.
   - *The Wreckers' Ledger*.
   - *The Band*: relay chatter, and the answer.

## 12. The wilderness world kind: the Long After

The wilderness world kind (generator 3) stays a separate legacy experience and gets no new
features; the canon binds its text only. The owner chose option A, "The Long After", on
2026-11-19: the wilderness lies beyond the Rim, generations after the fall.

- The evacuees' descendants were cut off when the Port failed and lost the Charter's
  technology. They live in **Holds**: villages, homesteads, timber castles and stone
  fortresses, armed with black powder under the Arms Act's long shadow. The Headhunters are
  raiders of the high forests.
- The wilderness's ancient ruins are Port-era ruins. Its relic weapons are old Warden issue.
  Its deep "resonant" crystal may be where the bore's singing came from (OPEN).
- The crash-landed survivor of that mode comes from the first ship in generations. The canon
  leaves open whether it was answering the old relay.

Option B, which kept the wilderness outside the canon, was not chosen.

## 13. Tone and writing rules

- Plain, concrete and human. A small detail beats a speech, for example a shopping list or a
  child's drawing taped to a door.
- Records are short: usually under 80 words, in one person's voice, dated "Day *N*" or "CY 61".
  Notes may speak to "whoever finds this", and never to a chosen one.
- No record explains the Veil fully. No record sends the reader to a particular container, and
  none points to an antiserum.
- Grief, stubbornness and dry humour are welcome. Gore is never there for show.
- Children are never shown or described as taken. They appear only through what they left
  behind.
- The world never contradicts §9.

## 14. Originality (urban R9)

The Last of Us and Project Zomboid are atmosphere references only.

- No names, places, brands, factions or text from them, or from any other zombie fiction.
- Avoid their signature imagery and beats:
  - fungal spores and gas masks as the core image;
  - a military exclusion zone with soldiers, helicopters and leaflets;
  - a scheduled television or radio broadcast timeline;
  - a "this is how you died" summary.
- VEYLON's sealed basin is a colony's own civil decision on an alien world. Its infection is an
  alien membrane from the deep. Its weapons follow the Arms Act.
