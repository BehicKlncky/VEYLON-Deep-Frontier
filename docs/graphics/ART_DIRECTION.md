# VEYLON — Art Direction

Stylized voxel terrain + articulated cuboid props/creatures. Low-res (64×64)
hand-noise textures, soft but directional lighting, restrained palette. Never
photoreal; silhouettes and color temperature do the storytelling.

## Visual language (color families)

| Domain | Palette | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | desaturated greens `#6a8f4f`, soil browns `#7a5a3a`, cold stone `#8a8c92` | never candy-saturated |
| Safe space / fire | warm amber `#ffb347`, orange `#ff7a1a` | campfires, torches, lit windows |
| Human tech | worn off-white `#d8d4c8`, dark gray `#3a3d42`, safety orange `#e8641e` accents | crash pods, scrap |
| Veylon tech / beacon | cyan `#39d0d8`, teal `#1e8f96` emissive | the "rescue" color |
| Ancient ruins | cold stone `#585c6e` + restrained violet `#8a6fd0` / cyan energy | resonant cores |
| Toxic / corrupted | yellow-green `#a8c832` | ONLY used for danger |
| Blood / injury | dark readable red `#8f1a1a` | not neon |
| Night | dark navy ambient, warm firelight contrast | navigable, silhouette-first |

## Biome identities

| Biome | Ground/rock | Vegetation silhouette | Fog/sky | Accent | Motif |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meadow | vivid-ish grass / gray stone | round leafy trees, tall grass | clear warm blue | wildflower white | open rolling hills |
| Pine Forest | dark grass / mossy stone | tall narrow pines | cool blue-green, denser fog | deep green | tree columns |
| Rocky Highlands | bare stone / gravel | sparse scrub | thin dry haze | slate blue | exposed rock strata |
| Wet Marsh | murky grass / clay | low bushes, reeds | green-gray mist | murky teal | standing water |
| Cold Ridge | snow / ice | frosted pines | pale white-blue, bright | ice cyan | snowfields |
| Dry Scrubland | sand / dried clay | thorn bushes | warm yellow haze | ochre | heat shimmer flats |

Foliage tint is biome+season driven (uniform, shader-side): Wet = lush,
Frost = desaturated gray-green + frost on top faces, Dry = yellowed.

## Simulation states must be visible

- **Wet**: top surfaces darken ~25% and get a specular kick while raining.
- **Snow/frost**: cold biomes/season whiten upward faces (shader accent).
- **Ashfall**: global desaturation + gray flakes + darker sky.
- **Toxic fog**: short yellow-green fog, sickly grade, floating motes.
- **Fire**: warm point light, smoke + embers, scorched (ASH) blocks stay black.
- **Night**: cool blue ambient; block lights are warm amber — warm/cool contrast.
- **Dawn/dusk**: low warm sun, long shadows, orange horizon band.

## Lighting rules

- One sun/moon directional + hemisphere ambient (sky color above, ground bounce
  below). Vertex AO everywhere. One PCF shadow map.
- Sun color: 5500K noon → 2600K at horizon; moon: dim steel blue.
- Block light (torch/fire/beacon) is warm `(1.0, 0.62, 0.28)` except beacon/core
  cyan handled by emissive textures.
- Exposure slightly darker at night; bloom only from genuine emissives.

## Restraint checklist (every feature must pass)

- Does it read at 100% UI scale, 720p, in fog, at night?  
- Is saturation ≤ the palette table?  
- Bloom only on emissives; fog never hides gameplay inside 12 m; camera effects
  optional and subtle.

## The abandoned city (urban milestone 22)

The city is restrained, lived-in and left: concrete, brick, plaster and sheet
metal, weathered rather than wrecked. Decay shows through a grade on surfaces and
sparse detail, never noise on every wall. One strong motif marks the quarantine.
Everything is original to VEYLON and generated by project code.

### Materials and weathering

- The urban materials keep their 64×64 procedural tiles and position-hash
  variants (clean, streaked or stained, damaged). Damage reads as soft peeling and
  spalling, never black-outlined blotches.
- A district grade multiplies city surfaces by a few percent (table below): no
  channel below 0.69 or above 1.04. A loose panel over a hidden cellar is graded
  and dressed exactly like the plaster around it.
- Interiors keep their own colours; only the flooded quarter's tide line and
  cellars, and the industrial yards' soot, follow people indoors.

### District identities

| District | Walls and ground | Dressing | Water, fog and light | What reads at night and in fog |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reclaimed residential | Warm brick; a mossy foot to every wall | Ivy columns climb from the pavement (about one facade column in seven); the odd notice; photographs and children's drawings in flats; mould at damp skirtings | Meadow climate; clear | Courtyard insects; ivy silhouettes |
| Commercial downtown | Grimy cool grey | Posters and official notices at eye height (one wall cell in ten) | Clear | Long sightlines; glass falling |
| Industrial yards | Soot and rust, darker and warmer, indoors too | Fences; chemical spills on the ground | Light haze | Glowing spills; metal grinding |
| Flooded quarter | A damp cool cast; a dark tide line on the first two cells of every wall, inside and out; water-stained cellars | Sparse ivy; mould in damp rooms | Murky, still water; mist | Lapping water |
| Civic precinct | The cleanest and coolest | Notices, a little ivy | Clear | Shutters and metal |
| Quarantine | A sickly wash, strongest at the foot of walls | The veil's growth climbs walls instead of ivy; the cordon mark on walls and on nearly one fence panel in three | Murk; toxic fog that sickens only here | Glowing contaminated ground and growth; cordon marks |

### The quarantine motif

The cordon mark is a hexagon in hazard yellow-green (`#a8c832`) with three
tendrils curling into its centre and a bar beneath, sprayed with overspray and
runs. Yellow-green stays reserved for danger: the mark appears only inside the
cordon, where the toxic fog sickens and contaminated ground lies. Where ivy would
climb elsewhere, the veil's own growth climbs the cordon's walls: the purple-grey,
pale-veined membrane of the growth sites.

### Signs, notices and traces of people

- Posters and notices carry no legible words in the world: coloured bands, a
  relay-mast pictogram, illegible type, stamps, tears and water stains.
- Personal traces are sparse, about one flat wall in forty at eye height: a
  framed photograph askew on its nail, a child's crayon drawing taped up. They
  are dressing, never containers or clues.
- Survivors leave marks beside street doors: a chalked ringed dot by a building
  that hides people, a painted arrow sending callers round to a camp's rear
  door, a red warning daubed by a raider yard. Looking at one reads its words on
  the interaction line. A mark hints; it never marks a map, finds a cellar or
  starts a mission, and strongholds keep their own signs.

### Proportions

Exteriors are the interiors seen from outside. The storey pitch is 4 blocks (a
slab and three cells of room), windows open from each storey's rooms at eye
height, ground floors meet the street at their doors, and basements sit one and
two pitches under the 40-block street datum. Dressing never adds geometry: it
lies on wall faces, so no facade shows a storey, window or door its plan does
not have.

### Light indoors and out

- A lamp, torch or fire lights what it can see. Walls, floors and closed doors
  stop it; doorways, open leaves and glass pass it. The renderer and every AI
  read the same light, so a lit patch never suggests an opening that is not
  there, and darkness hides a survivor from an infected only where it is dark.
- Rooms above the street take a little of the day through their windows (a
  small bounce on the ambient term); cellars and the deep fill stay dark and want
  a lamp or torch.
- Rain darkens and glosses, and frost whitens, only what the open sky reaches. A
  floor beside a window stays dry, as the simulation keeps it.
- Hazards are visible. Contaminated ground's blooms glow faintly. Toxic fog's
  sickly grade, motes and choking vignette show only where it sickens; elsewhere
  it is a thin grey haze.
- Hostile tells survive the dark. The veil through a Veilbound or Breacher glows
  faintly and brightens through a strike's windup, as a Veilmaw's veins do
  through its telegraph.
- City water is murky and still.

### Budgets

- Texture layers: 143 of 160. Package 22 added 13 tiles: 10 wall decals and 3
  door marks.
- Decals: at most 384 per chunk mesh, thin quads 0.012 blocks off their wall,
  with no entity, collider, light or save field of their own.
- The restraint checklist above applies: read at 720p, at night and in fog.
